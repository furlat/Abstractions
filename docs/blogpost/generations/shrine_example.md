### A Concrete Shrine Example

We now ground the discussion in a simple shrine that produces agentic traces at the turn scale. There are two gods that can be on duty at any given turn. Bird prefers Apples. Monkey prefers Bananas. The priest chooses an offering $a_t \in {\text{Apple}, \text{Banana}}$. The shrine responds by lighting exactly one of two lamps $o_t \in {\text{BirdLamp}, \text{MonkeyLamp}}$.

We fix a label mapping between offerings and lamps. Apple corresponds to BirdLamp. Banana corresponds to MonkeyLamp. The shrine’s response depends on which god is on duty. If Bird is on duty, the lamp matches the offering’s label. If Monkey is on duty, the lamp is the opposite of the offering’s label. The priest never sees which god is on duty. The only observables are the offering the priest made and the lamp that lit.

We make the duty schedule depend on whether the current god was pleased. Bird is pleased by Apple. Monkey is pleased by Banana. If the priest pleases the current god at turn $t$, then the other god takes the next shift. If the priest displeases the current god, the same god stays on duty. This choice gives a unifilar environment update at the turn level. Once we know which god was on duty, the offering, and the lamp that lit, the next duty state is uniquely determined by the pleased or displeased rule.

To connect with learning from data, we introduce a young apprentice who observes a long trace of turns generated by a drunk teacher. The teacher uses a state covering policy. The older priest got disillusioned with reality and gave into drinking, he now only does random offering not caring about pleasing the gods anymore. The randomness ensures that all relevant branches of the turn process are visited often enough. The apprentice only sees the sequence $Z={z_t}_{t=1}^T$ with $z_t=(a_t,o_t)$. There is no supervision about which god was on duty.


We keep the agent side simple. The policy’s objective is to satisfy the god in a way that alternates the lamps as often as possible. Intuitively this drives the observable process toward high entropy at the lamp level and keeps the turn process informative. In practice this means the policy tries to pick the offering that both pleases the current god and induces frequent swaps of the duty state, so that BirdLamp and MonkeyLamp appear in a balanced, rapidly changing pattern.


This setup fits the POMDP frame from the previous sections. The environment has a hidden state $s_t$ indicating which god is on duty. The agent has an internal state $\hat s_t$ that supports action selection. The joint turn probability factors as
$$
P(z_{t+1} \mid s_t, \hat s_t; \theta) = \pi(a_{t+1} \mid \hat s_t; \theta_\pi), P(o_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_{t+1}) .
$$
The hidden states update according to
$$
P(s_{t+1}, \hat s_{t+1} \mid s_t, \hat s_t, z_{t+1}; \theta) = P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_{t+1}, o_{t+1}), \mathcal{M}(\hat s_{t+1} \mid \hat s_t, a_{t+1}, o_{t+1}; \theta_{\mathcal{M}}) .
$$
The unifilar environment update implies that as turns accumulate the belief over the duty state concentrates along the branch consistent with the observed offerings and lamps.

From the apprentice’s perspective the task is pure next turn prediction. Train an autoregressor $G(\hat z_{t+1} \mid z_{\le t}; \theta_G)$ on the teacher’s dataset. Use the natural two step factorization at the turn boundary. First predict the action logits for $a_{t+1}$ given the history. Then condition on the sampled or teacher action and predict the lamp logits for $o_{t+1}$. The internal state right after emitting $a_{t+1}$ aligns with the agent slice that is useful for the policy. The internal state right after receiving $o_{t+1}$ aligns with the environment slice that is useful for predicting the next lamp. Because the environment update at the turn scale is synchronizable from $(a_{t+1},o_{t+1})$, the autoregressor can compress the past into a predictive state $q_t$ that is sufficient for both factors. In the finite state case we care about, the smallest such predictive state coincides with the minimal unifilar presentation at the turn scale.

The drunk teacher matters because it produces a state covering trace. The occasional random offering decorrelates the teacher’s action logits from any single lamp pattern and forces the dataset to include both pleased and displeased steps, both swaps and stays, both lamp outcomes conditioned on both offerings. As a result the apprentice can learn a predictive state that generalizes beyond the teacher’s idiosyncrasies. When we evaluate the apprentice, we can check two things. First, action prediction accuracy under the teacher’s trace. Second, observation prediction accuracy under counterfactual actions sampled from the apprentice’s own action head. Under the unifilar assumption both improve with the same internal predictive state.

Finally, we can put a reward on top of the shrine. Reward $+1$ for pleasing the current god. The policy that alternates the lamps as often as possible while keeping the lamp distribution balanced maximizes the entropy of the observable process under this generator and yields steady reward. The world model provides the informational substrate by keeping track of the duty state implicitly through the predictive state. The policy uses that state to select offerings that satisfy the current god and induce frequent swaps of the duty state. The apprentice learns both from the same agentic traces. The internal state it develops supports the two natural boundaries of the turn, at action time and at observation time, and is sufficient for reward maximization in this shrine.
